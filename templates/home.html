About Project ARUGA

Development and Evaluation of ARUGA: An Intelligent Assistive Healthcare Robot for Vital-Sign Monitoring, Medication Support, and Blood-Pressure Trend-Based Hypertension Risk Identification for Elderly People — is a research initiative developed at Muntinlupa National High School (STEM Strand).

As more elderly live independently, many face difficulties in regularly monitoring their health, managing medication routines, and recognizing early changes in their physical condition. ARUGA addresses these challenges by combining an IoT-based assistive companion robot, a wearable sensor wristband, and a web-based monitoring platform into one integrated and affordable healthcare support system.

The system continuously monitors heart rate, blood oxygen saturation (SpO2), and internal temperature, while blood pressure is estimated from physiological sensor data. These current readings are stored together with historical records, allowing ARUGA to review previous measurements and identify changes or patterns over time. Using these historical vital-sign and blood-pressure records, the system applies ARIMA time-series forecasting to generate future readings and short-term health trends.

Through this approach, ARUGA does not rely only on the user's present condition, but also considers both past and predicted future blood-pressure behavior. This allows the system to provide early warning indications of possible hypertension risk based on estimated blood-pressure patterns, trend movement, and forecasted values. In this way, ARUGA supports a more proactive and preventive approach to health monitoring rather than waiting for critical conditions to occur.

In addition to health monitoring, ARUGA also supports smart medication assistance, remote monitoring, and mobile robotic navigation, helping users and authorized caregivers maintain safer and more consistent daily healthcare routines.

How ARUGA Works

Wearable Wristband Unit

Powered by an ESP32 micro-controller, incorporating MAX30102 light sensors for non-invasive health tracking.

Companion Robot Base

Driven by an ESP32 microcontroller managing the physical medicine dispenser, LCD display, local buzzers, and ESP32-CAM.

GSM Emergency Gateway

Uses a SIM800L module to ensure critical alerts reach caregivers instantly via SMS, even without active home internet.
